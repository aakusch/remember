# User research process

How we plan, run, and synthesize customer research. The goal is decisions, not artifacts — every research effort produces a written recommendation, not a 40-page deck.

## Cadence

- **Continuous discovery interviews** — every PM/UX pair runs 3 interviews per week, no exceptions
- **Targeted research sprints** — 2-3 week deep dives before major commitments (new product area, big bet)
- **Quarterly NPS verbatim review** — pulls qualitative signal from NPS responses

## Recruiting

We maintain a customer research panel with ~400 active opted-in customers across our segments. Recruit from there first.

For prospects (non-customers), we use UserInterviews.com with the role/company filters we've tuned over time.

## Interview protocol

Standard flow:

1. **Warmup** (5 min) — let them describe their role and current process
2. **Specific recall** (15-20 min) — walk through the last time they did X, in detail. We're after stories, not opinions.
3. **Speculative future** (10 min) — only if we have specific concepts to validate. Keep it light; people lie about hypotheticals.
4. **Wrap-up** (5 min) — anything they wanted to bring up that we didn't get to

Total 30-45 min. Two people on the call: one drives, one takes notes.

## Synthesis

After each interview:

- Notes go into the research database (Notion → research/interviews/{date}-{name})
- Quotes that capture a verbatim insight get tagged with the topic
- Within 24 hours

After a research sprint (5-10 interviews):

- Affinity clustering of quotes
- Identify patterns that show up in at least 3 interviews
- Write a 1-2 page recommendation with: what we learned, what we should do, what we should not do, what we still don't know

## What this isn't

- **Not validation theater** — we don't run research to confirm decisions we've already made
- **Not a feature factory feed** — the goal is to understand the problem space, not to source feature requests
- **Not a substitute for usage data** — what people do beats what they say

## Tools

- Calendly for scheduling
- Loom for async sharing
- Notion for the research database
- Otter.ai for transcripts (with consent)
